---
title: Signals and outcomes
description: Attach human feedback, corrections, rewards, tool results, sensors, and delayed business outcomes.
---

A signal is evidence observed during or after an execution. It targets one execution or an entire episode.

## Signal kinds

- <code>rating</code>
- <code>correction</code>
- <code>outcome</code>
- <code>reward</code>
- <code>approval</code>
- <code>tool_result</code>
- <code>custom</code>

## Fields

| Field | Required | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| namespace | yes | Must match the targeted execution and isolation boundary |
| executionId or episodeId | one | At least one attribution target is required |
| kind | yes | Evidence category |
| name | yes | Application metric, such as resolved, operator_correct, latency_ms, or glucose_delta |
| value | yes | JSON-safe observed value |
| correction | no | Verified replacement answer, label, route, intent, tool, or structured target |
| source | yes | Operator, customer, evaluator, sensor, tool, or business system |
| confidence | no | Evidence weight from 0 to 1; defaults to 1 |
| metadata | no | Collection method, reason code, validation state, or source version |
| observedAt | no | When the evidence became available |

## Evidence hierarchy

1. **Verified correction or real-world outcome.** A confirmed label, durable resolution, successful action, sensor result, or business outcome gives an actionable target.
2. **Approval plus successful execution.** Approval is useful when downstream behavior confirms the plan worked.
3. **Explicit rating.** A rating prioritizes a segment but often does not identify the correct behavior.
4. **Implicit behavior.** Restatement, abandonment, or silence can help discovery but should not become training truth without verification.

## Direct and delayed attribution

Use <code>executionId</code> when evidence belongs to one model call, retrieval, or tool. Use <code>episodeId</code> when the result belongs to the whole task. Episode outcomes can arrive hours or days later.

When analysis selects only root turns, an episode outcome is attributed to those selected executions rather than duplicated across every child operation.

## A critical training rule

Never train directly on a disliked output. Pair negative evidence with an authoritative correction or omit it from supervised training. A negative signal says something went wrong; it does not say what the model should learn.

---
title: Signals and outcomes
---

Signals are immutable ratings, corrections, outcomes, rewards, approvals, tool results
or custom observations. Every signal needs `name`, `value`, `source`, and an
`executionId` or `episodeId`. A direct execution reference must already exist in the
same namespace. Episode-only outcomes may arrive before all episode executions.

The SDK verifies shape and scope, not the authority of `source`. Your application
must decide which operators can verify a correction. Rephrasing a question, clicking
thumbs-down or observing a glucose spike is not automatically a causal ground-truth
label. High-risk applications require domain-specific safeguards outside this SDK.

Use a stable **semantic signal ID**, such as the upstream review-event ID. An identical
ID/input retry is idempotent; conflicting input fails. Distinct IDs with identical
text are not automatically deduplicated: they might be genuinely different reviews.
Aggregation deduplicates the same ID before counting and normalizes within episodes.

`confidence` is an evidence weight between zero and one, not a calibrated probability.
Zero-weight signals may be stored for audit, but do not contribute effective support,
score, recurrence or effect. Finite booleans/numbers and common success/failure strings
have default scores. Other values are unscored unless your synchronous score callback
returns a finite number. Use consistent scales and filter `signalNames`/`signalKinds`.

`observedAt` describes when the outcome was observed, independently of execution time.
Analysis accepts `executionWindow` and `observationWindow` separately, each with
inclusive `from` and exclusive `to`. Leave the execution window broad enough to admit
the older executions referenced by delayed outcomes.

Conflicting verified corrections are not resolved by voting automatically. The
classification starter excludes executions with multiple distinct verified labels
from proposal examples. Resolve disputes through your own review workflow and
explicitly version the resulting evidence; never silently overwrite an earlier signal.
